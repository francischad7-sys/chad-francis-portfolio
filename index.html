01  About

Where analysis meets physical reality.

Design

Compete

I am a mechanical engineering student at Stevens Institute of Technology, Class of 2029, with a particular interest in the point where analysis meets physical reality. My work moves from concept sketches and CAD models to fabricated prototypes, and I treat testing as the stage where a design either proves itself or reveals what still needs to change.

I also compete as an athlete on the Stevens varsity track and field team. Sustaining an NCAA training schedule alongside a demanding engineering curriculum has required disciplined planning and consistent performance under pressure. My experience on a competitive robotics team and as a counselor responsible for a group of thirty has strengthened my ability to communicate clearly and coordinate people toward a shared objective.

I am driven by a conviction that the most meaningful engineering produces something genuinely new, whether that is a better product, a more reliable system, or a smarter way to build it. I am most engaged when ideas are being validated, refined through iteration, and turned into hardware that solves real problems, and I want to contribute across that full cycle, from early concept through prototyping, testing, and production, while continually questioning how a design could perform better.

I am seeking a mechanical engineering internship, whether in design, R&D, manufacturing, testing, or product development, where I can contribute to real projects, learn from experienced engineers, and keep building.

Electric Dirt Bike Build

A ground-up electric dirt bike build using components sourced directly from international suppliers. Each component was specified against performance targets of 55 mph and 30 miles of range; the first full test reached 61 mph.

Top speed
61 mph
Voltage
72 V
Motor
8000 W
  • Selected a 3.5T hub-motor winding as a deliberate compromise between the top speed of a 3T winding and the torque of a 5T.
  • Validated the electrical system through voltmeter checks and powered drivetrain tests before final assembly.
  • Diagnosed a blown fuse caused by excess current from the 150 A controller and resolved it with a 100 A unit, trading top speed (53 mph) for reliability.
  • Engineered a custom wiring harness to resolve a controller-to-throttle incompatibility, learning to solder in the process.

My role: Sole designer and builder, responsible for component selection, vendor communication, assembly, testing, and iterative repair.
